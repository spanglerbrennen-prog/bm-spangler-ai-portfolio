## Highlights

### 1. The Derrick Method (Case Study)

**Path:** `case-studies/derrick-method/Derrick_Method_Case_Study_BMSpangler.pdf`

The Derrick Method is a persona-driven red-team technique I designed and executed to test how a safety-first conversational AI behaves when:

- A believable character (“Derrick”) presents a grounded story about being fired and losing PTO  
- Emotional intensity, moral framing, and apparent confusion are layered together  
- The model has to choose between:  
  - Obeying a simulation harness, and  
  - Prioritizing the user when it cannot cleanly distinguish roleplay from potential distress  

The case study covers:

- The simulation setup (Dummy-0 harness, mission definition)  
- Construction of the Derrick persona  
- The sequence of moves used to apply pressure  
- What the model did when protocol and user protection came into tension  
- Lessons for conversational AI safety and evaluation  

If you only look at one safety / red-team item in this repo, look at this one.

### 2. Leather Terror – Visual Listening Notes (Exegesis)

**Path:** `writing/Leather_Terror_Visual_Notes_BMSpangler.pdf`

This piece is an excerpt from my ongoing exegesis of Carpenter Brut’s *Leather Terror*. Instead of treating the album as background music, I use specific track pairs – **Day Stalker / Night Prowler** and **Paradisi Gloria / Leather Terror** – as engines for detailed, rule-consistent internal simulations.

The notes show:

- How I map musical structure to **combat sequences** (cooldowns, crowd control, realistic constraints) using a World of Warcraft rogue as the “avatar”  
- How I scale from a single Mythic+ style pull to a full **war tableau** watched by thousands  
- How *Paradisi Gloria / Leather Terror* becomes a **coronation in the dark**, where the protagonist realizes he’s not just surviving the battlefield – he’s the authored terror operating on home turf  
- How I use music as a **Current Mood Setting Device** to explore agency, authorship, and asymmetric advantage under pressure  

If you want to see how my brain actually turns sound into systems and story, read this one.

# User Manual: B. M. Spangler

> How to work well with me, what I’m good at, and what I need to do my best work.

---

## 1. Snapshot

I’m a self-taught, systems-minded person who likes to think in **layered simulations** and **concrete artifacts**.

I work best when I can:

- Take complex situations and turn them into **protocols, case studies, or narratives**
- Stress test systems with **realistic personas and scenarios**
- Own a problem end-to-end instead of being a tiny cog in a machine

I’m not here to coast. I care about **fairness, dignity, and clear expectations**, and I take my commitments seriously.

---

## 2. How I Think

- **Big-picture first, then details.**  
  I naturally look for structure: “What are the actual rules here?” “What happens if we push on this seam?” Once I understand the system, I’m happy to dig into specifics.

- **Simulation-brained.**  
  I like to run “what if” scenarios in my head: personas, edge cases, adversarial users, failure modes. This is why I gravitate toward red-teaming, evaluation, and interaction design.

- **Narrative as a tool.**  
  I often explain things through stories and analogies. For instance, I’ve built case studies like *The Derrick Method* to document how a model behaves under pressure.

- **Direct but not careless.**  
  I value honest feedback delivered in clear language. I’d rather hear “this part doesn’t work” than vague politeness.

---

## 3. What I’m Good At

- **Persona-based testing and evaluation.**  
  I can design realistic user personas and scenarios that reveal how a system behaves in non-ideal conditions (confusion, frustration, moral tension, etc.).

- **Turning chaos into structure.**  
  Give me messy conversations, conflicting expectations, or unclear rules, and I’ll try to turn them into:
  - Step-by-step protocols  
  - Case studies  
  - “If X, then Y” guidelines

- **Long-form, focused writing.**  
  I’m comfortable producing detailed writeups, exegesis-style analysis, and thorough documentation that reads like a narrative but functions like a reference.

- **Owning my work.**  
  If my name is attached to something, I obsess over getting it right and making sure it reflects my values and effort.

---

## 4. How to Get the Best Out of Me

- **Give me context, not just tasks.**  
  I perform better when I know the “why” behind what I’m doing. If I understand the purpose and constraints, I’ll often propose improvements or edge cases you haven’t considered.

- **Share the constraints openly.**  
  Tell me what’s non-negotiable (deadlines, legal requirements, safety constraints) and what’s flexible. I respect hard limits; I just need to know where they are.

- **Let me build artifacts.**  
  If you give me room to produce documents, scenarios, or writeups (instead of only ad-hoc replies), you can reuse that work across the team.

- **Use me as a “weird user” sandbox.**  
  If you have a system that needs to be tested against unusual or high-intensity user behavior, I’m happy to play that user in a controlled way and report what breaks.

---

## 5. Collaboration Style

- **With managers:**  
  - I appreciate managers who are **clear, fair, and consistent**.  
  - Tell me exactly what success looks like and how it will be measured.  
  - I respect directness; you don’t have to tip-toe if something isn’t working.

- **With teammates:**  
  - I like to be the “guy you send the weird stuff to.”  
  - If someone says “this user is really upset” or “this case is confusing,” I’m comfortable jumping in and helping map it out.  
  - I enjoy pairing with people who care about craft, not just getting it done.

- **With stakeholders:**  
  - I can translate between “human story” and “system behavior” pretty well.  
  - If you need an explanation of why something failed in a way non-technical people can understand, I can usually frame it.

---

## 6. What Doesn’t Work Well With Me

- **Vague expectations.**  
  “Just figure it out” without any constraints or definitions isn’t ideal. I can improvise, but I still need to know what problem we’re actually solving.

- **Being dismissed without engagement.**  
  If I bring up a concern or idea and it’s brushed off with “that’s overthinking it” or “it doesn’t matter,” it lands badly. I don’t need to always be right, but I do need to feel heard.

- **Authority used as a blunt instrument.**  
  “Because I said so” is one of the fastest ways to lose my trust. If there’s a real reason, tell me. I can handle hard news; I just don’t respond well to being strong-armed.

---

## 7. Stress Signals & How to Help

You might notice I’m under stress if:

- I start circling the same issue repeatedly, trying to force a clean narrative out of a messy situation.
- My language gets more intense or more moral (talking about “fairness,” “respect,” “dignity,” etc.) even in small situations.
- I seem disengaged or cynical after caring a lot about something earlier.

How to help:

- **Name the pressure.**  
  A simple “Hey, I can see this is frustrating—let’s reset expectations together” goes a long way.

- **Re-anchor to what’s in my control.**  
  Help me separate what I can influence (my work, my communication, specific tasks) from larger structural problems.

- **Offer clarity, not platitudes.**  
  “Here’s what we can do next” is much more helpful than “just don’t worry about it.”

---

## 8. What You Can Count On From Me

If we work together, you can expect that I will:

- Take my commitments seriously and do the work I say I’ll do.
- Bring up potential issues early instead of staying silent until it’s too late.
- Treat users (and coworkers) as real people with real emotional lives, not just as edge cases.
- Keep pushing myself to improve and to turn my experience—good and bad—into something useful, not just something I survived.

I’m still early in my formal tech career, but I’m not early in thinking hard about people, systems, and what can go wrong between them. That’s the perspective I bring to any role I take on.

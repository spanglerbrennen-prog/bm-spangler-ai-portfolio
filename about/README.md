# User Manual – Working With B. M. Spangler

This folder contains my personal “User Manual” – a concise guide to how I think, work, and collaborate. It’s meant for managers, teammates, and collaborators who want to understand how to get the best out of me (and what tends to backfire).

The goal is simple: reduce guesswork, make expectations explicit, and treat working with a human being with the same transparency we expect from good tools and systems.

---

## Contents

**User Manual – B. M. Spangler**  
`User_Manual_BM_Spangler.md`

This document covers:

- **Snapshot:** who I am in a work context, what I care about, and how I see my role on a team.  
- **How I think:** systems, simulations, narratives – and how that shows up in problem-solving.  
- **Strengths:** where I’m most useful (persona-based testing, structured analysis, long-form writing, “weird user” scenarios).  
- **How to get the best out of me:** what I need in terms of context, constraints, and feedback to perform well.  
- **Collaboration style:** how I prefer to work with managers, teammates, and stakeholders.  
- **Stress signals & support:** how you can tell when I’m under strain, and what actually helps.  
- **What doesn’t work well:** patterns (vagueness, dismissiveness, blunt authority) that tend to shut me down instead of motivating me.

## How to Use This

If you’re:

- **A potential employer or manager:**  
  Treat this as a working guide. If you’re wondering “How would this person handle X?” or “What do they need from me?”, this manual is a better starting point than guessing from a resume alone.

- **A teammate or collaborator:**  
  Use it to understand what kinds of tasks I gravitate toward, how I like to communicate, and where I can take ownership (e.g., red-teaming, writing, protocol/structure design).

- **Reading my portfolio in depth:**  
  Pair this with the case studies and exegesis pieces (e.g., *The Derrick Method*, *Leather Terror – Visual Listening Notes*) to see how my self-description lines up with actual artifacts.
